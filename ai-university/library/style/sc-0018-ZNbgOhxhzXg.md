# Hook (0:00–0:40)
Cold-demo hook using the video itself as proof: the creator points to on-screen text, subtitles, motion graphics, and a repositioned face-cam that were all made by the tool being taught, with a hyperbolic title claim ("destroyed every editing tool"). Promises the viewer will learn to do exactly this and will get a free repo/skill to replicate the setup. The medium is the message — the polished video is the argument.

# Beat map
Long-form (32 min) show-then-teach, split into two methods. Opening montage of finished examples → contextualizing the manual effort saved → Method 1: a hosted design app (examples, limitations, a live build) → Method 2: a code-based framework (examples including failures, setup via a coding agent, a full live build with multiple feedback-and-render iterations) → cost/context/hardware notes → outro with free-resource CTA.

# Retention devices
- Front-loaded reel of impressive outputs sets a "can I really do that?" curiosity gap.
- Honest inclusion of failed/weak attempts (a static product demo, a not-yet-postable short) builds trust and resets expectations without losing momentum.
- Iteration suspense: repeated V1→V2→V3→V4 render cycles with visible fixes create mini-cliffhangers ("fingers crossed").
- Recurring "this would've taken hours by hand" reframing keeps perceived value high across a long runtime.
- Practical asides (token usage checks, RAM warnings) add credibility and keep advanced viewers engaged.

# CTA placement
Repeated but soft: the free GitHub repo/skill via the community is mentioned early (as the payoff for watching) and again at the end; a brief tool-in-description mention mid-video. Given the length, the resource offer is reinforced more than once but never becomes a hard interruptive sell; standard like/outro closes.
