# Hook (0:00–~1:00)
Authority + contrarian promise. Leads with scale credibility (built hundreds of
workflows, taught thousands) then a pattern-interrupt claim: everyone online builds the
fancy stuff, but businesses only actually want five simple, boring automations — and
those pay the most. Promises to name all five plus why they sell and how to sell them.
The "boring beats fancy" reframe is the retention bait.

# Beat map
- ~0:00–1:00 Hook + credibility + the "five simple things" promise + how I know this.
- ~1:00–3:30 Automation 1 (speed-to-lead): what it is, who pays, a worked value example
  with concrete numbers.
- ~3:30–5:30 Automation 2 (document processing): the manual-pain picture, the fix, the
  cost math, note that it's often rule-based/no-AI.
- ~5:30–7:00 Bridge: explicitly warns the next two sound similar but differ.
- ~7:00–9:30 Automation 3 (follow-up/nurture) with a webinar-funnel value example.
- ~9:30–11:30 Automation 4 (database reactivation) with a gym value example.
- ~11:30–13:00 Automation 5 (internal reporting) + a real "simple but profitable" mini
  case.
- ~13:00–14:30 How to sell: outcome-not-workflow; specialist vs consultant; the
  find-the-bottleneck question and the flow analogy.
- ~14:30–14:51 Resource offer + next-video pointer + like ask.

# Retention devices
- Numbered listicle spine (five items) with clear progress, each a self-contained payoff.
- Every item follows the same micro-structure (what → who pays → worked value math),
  training the viewer to anticipate the payoff.
- Concrete invented dollar figures per example make abstract ROI vivid.
- Explicit "these two sound alike but differ, pay attention" callout pre-empts confusion
  and re-engages.
- Two teaching analogies (a specialist eatery for focus; a clogged pipe for constraints)
  anchor the selling section.
- Saves "the most underrated one" for last to hold viewers to the end.

# CTA placement
No hard mid-roll; content runs uninterrupted through all five items. End CTA offers a
free resource guide in the community summarizing the framework, an on-screen next-video
suggestion, and the standard like ask. CTA is value-additive (a guide), matching the
educational tone.
