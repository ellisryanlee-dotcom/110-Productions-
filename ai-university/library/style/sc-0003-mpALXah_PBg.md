# Hook (0:00–~2:00)
Pattern: bold transformation promise ("complete beginner to pro") → reassurance ("even if you've never touched it") → tangible payoff list (build automations, websites, apps, your own AI executive assistant) → "laid out in the exact order I'd want to learn this" → a fast 24-chapter agenda flythrough. Authority via ordering and comprehensiveness rather than a cold demo. Each chapter later re-opens with its own mini-hook.

# Beat map
A 24-chapter mega-course of stacked, self-contained lessons, roughly grouped:
- Framing: the agentic market shift; self-healing while building vs. deterministic once deployed.
- Setup & foundations: five ways to run the tool, cost/ROI, prompting, permission modes, model families, tokens/context, CLAUDE.md.
- First builds: agentic automations via the WAT framework (newsletter, competitor analysis, job scraper); deploy 24/7 (Modal / trigger.dev).
- Architecture & context: project structure, built-in commands, context management.
- Capabilities: RAG (multimodal embeddings), websites, workflows-into-apps, APIs, MCP, Google Workspace CLI.
- Advanced agents: executive assistant, skills, sub-agents, agent teams, browser automation.
- Craft: permissions, GitHub, work trees, then "fun hacks."
- Close: monetization (positioning, value-based pricing).
Nearly every chapter follows: concept slides → live build → recap → CTA.

# Retention devices
- Live, unscripted building where errors surface and are debugged on camera, framed as proof of self-healing and as "so you don't bang your head against the wall."
- Repeated free-artifact drops (CLAUDE.md files, skills, prompts, templates) per chapter.
- Frequent reassurance for non-technical viewers ("IDE stands for… but you don't need to know that") lowering intimidation.
- Analogies to make abstract ideas concrete (recipe/ingredients for the framework, notepad for the context window, a universal port for MCP, onboarding a hire for context) — used then quickly returned to the build.
- Forward teasers ("stick around for the one that excites me most") and constant recaps of what was covered.
- Multi-monitor / parallel-agent demos to convey leverage and speed.

# CTA placement
- Recurring low-friction CTA to a free community to grab each chapter's resource (tied to the artifact, repeated at chapter starts/ends).
- Affiliate/tool CTAs woven in naturally at the moment a tool is introduced (hosting, scraping, voice-to-text), often with a discount code.
- Softer paid-community CTA at natural breaks after big builds.
- Standard like/subscribe reserved for chapter and course ends.
- Funnel order is consistent: deliver value/artifact → free community → paid community; the ask rides on top of teaching, never replacing it.
