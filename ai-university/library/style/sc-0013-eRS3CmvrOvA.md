# Hook (0:00–0:30)
Authority-plus-curation opener: cites a large hours-invested number for credibility, then a counterintuitive claim (the flashy skills people build aren't what businesses want — they want six boring, effective ones). Promises to reveal what they are, why they sell, and how to use them to make money, and teases a "how I figured this out" credibility section before the list.

# Beat map
Curated countdown (six main picks + a bonus seventh) over ~13 min. Structure per item is consistent: name the skill → the problem it solves → who pays for it / why it sells → install note. A short mid-list aside clarifies terminology (skill vs. plugin). Ends with a "how to actually sell these" business section, then resource-guide offer and outro.

# Retention devices
- Numbered list with a known endpoint (six) plus a surprise bonus item extends the loop past the expected finish.
- Repeated per-item template (problem → who pays → install) sets a rhythm that's easy to follow.
- Business-outcome framing on every item ("clients will pay most for these") keeps stakes tied to money.
- Callback structure — each later skill positioned relative to earlier ones (process vs. context vs. memory) — rewards continuous viewing.
- A brief on-camera aside (addressing an "are you an AI avatar" comment) breaks pattern and adds human texture.

# CTA placement
Value-first: teaching runs uninterrupted; a single end offer of a free resource guide via the community, a pointer to a related video, then the standard like-and-outro. Install commands are pushed to the description rather than read aloud, keeping the body clean.
