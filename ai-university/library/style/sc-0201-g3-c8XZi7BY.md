# Hook (0:00–~3:00)
Pattern: cold montage of authority-figure soundbites (tech CEOs asserting an agent revolution) → stacked shock claims (mass job loss, a familiar device "dying," "most people have no idea") → direct "but you're different" flattery → explicit promise of what the next ~60 minutes delivers (what the technology is, how it evolves, how to position yourself) → stakes framing ("your survival guide," "most important video you'll watch"). No demo; the hook is a fear-plus-opportunity promise anchored to borrowed authority.

# Beat map
Long-form video essay, roughly:
- Vision cold-open: a detailed near-future day-in-the-life scene to make the abstract tangible.
- Framing section: reframes an everyday tool category around a single limiting factor, then walks a decades-long historical trend to argue the shift is inevitable.
- Four-phase evolution model of the core technology (each phase = same underlying system, only the input method changes), presented in escalating order.
- Consequences act, split three ways: personal life, work life, business/customer relationships — each with an illustrative future scenario built around a named persona.
- Positioning act: audience segmented into four groups, each given a distinct strategy and a "winners vs. losers" contrast.
- Close: restate stakes, "which side of history" rhetorical question, one final borrowed-authority soundbite.

# Retention devices
- Cinematic future-scenario storytelling to open and to illustrate each major section (concrete persona + sensory detail).
- Borrowed authority: interwoven clips of famous founders/executives to validate each claim.
- Sequential "phases" framework that promises escalation, creating an open loop to the final phase.
- Repeated forward-references ("more on this later," "we'll break this down next").
- Recurring "winners vs. losers / prepare vs. deny" contrast to keep stakes high.
- Segment-by-segment recaps before advancing.

# CTA placement
- Soft, woven CTAs to a free community/course dropped at natural topic breaks (especially in the "how to position yourself" segment, tied to learning the skills).
- No hard product pitch; the ask is framed as the logical next step for the audience segment being addressed.
- Standard subscribe reserved for the end.
