# Hook (0:00–~0:45)
Result-first curiosity gap. Leads with a concrete, slightly provocative outcome (built
a simple thing in a couple hours, got paid a specific sum), immediately undercuts the
mystique ("it wasn't complex," "I'd do it in 30 minutes now"), then makes it relatable
("you could build this too") and lays out the agenda: what it did, why they paid, what
it means for you, and how I'd start over. The number + "and it's simpler than you
think" is the retention bait.

# Beat map
- ~0:00–0:45 Hook + agenda.
- ~0:45–3:00 What the system actually was (reframes "agent" as a simple workflow) and
  self-deprecating context about being an early, non-expert builder.
- ~3:00–5:00 Why the client paid: the origin story (content → inbound → casual call →
  collaboration → price) and the trust/authenticity thesis.
- ~5:00–7:00 The pricing lesson: paying for outcome not hours; how to compute value.
- ~7:00–9:30 "If I started from zero today" playbook: share your journey, learn to
  diagnose problems, broaden tool literacy, prototype fast, price on ROI.
- ~9:30–10:24 Soft resource offer + audience question + like/CTA.

# Retention devices
- Specific dollar figures as recurring anchors ("$1,200," "600/hour," "10x").
- Reframes that subvert expectations ("it's not even an agent") keep the viewer
  re-evaluating.
- Story structure (a personal narrative arc) carries the middle instead of screen demo,
  since this is a talking/lesson video, not a build.
- Named mental models delivered as quotable one-liners (diagnose-don't-just-implement;
  make the offer a no-brainer) act as memorable checkpoints.
- Forward promises ("here's exactly what I'd do") open a loop paid off in the playbook.

# CTA placement
No hard mid-roll. Light resource offer near the end (a doc summarizing the video in the
free community) plus an engagement prompt asking if viewers want more selling-focused
content, then the standard like ask. CTA is consultative, matching the business topic.
