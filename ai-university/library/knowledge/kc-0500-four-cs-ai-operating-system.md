# The Four C's for building an AI operating system

**What:** A four-stage, ordered framework for standing up a personal or business "AI operating system" — a workspace where an AI harness (like a coding agent) becomes the default place you do work. The stages, taken in order, are Context, Connections, Capabilities, and Cadence. The first two build the "second brain" (knowledge); the last two build the operating system on top of it (action).

**Why it matters:** Most people bolt AI onto scattered subscriptions and custom chatbots, repeat themselves constantly, and never get an assistant that actually knows their world. A staged order prevents this: you can't automate meaningfully until the agent has real context and live data. It's also a teachable sequence you can use to onboard a team or sell setups to a business.

**The moves:**
1. **Context** — load static knowledge about who you and your business are (background, goals, processes, achievements). This is the routing/reference layer the agent reads first.
2. **Connections** — wire in live, changing data through APIs/CLIs (email, calendar, CRM, financials, task manager, meeting transcripts). Static context plus live connections = a "second brain."
3. **Capabilities** — build skills, agents, automations, and pipelines that let the system *do* work, not just answer.
4. **Cadence** — promote proven capabilities to run on triggers (manual, event, or schedule) so work happens unattended.

**Watch out for:** Take the stages in order; skipping to automation before context/connections exist produces a system that behaves like a stranger. As you add autonomy, cost, risk, and maintenance all rise — cadence must be *earned* by battle-testing capabilities first.

**Original example to invent:** The source frames this for a solo creator's business. Writers should invent a different domain (e.g., a freelance bookkeeper or a small landscaping firm) and walk each C with domain-specific data sources and automations.
