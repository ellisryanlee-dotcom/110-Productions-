# The freelancer-to-agency climb and its growing pains

**What:** Moving from solo freelancer to consultant to agency raises what you can
charge but adds operational drag: more staff to coordinate, extra moving parts, and
wider openings for inefficiency. Early on, senior people end up doing work below
their role (e.g., a technical lead stuck building instead of leading engineers)
because systems haven't caught up to project size. Maturity looks like each person
in their right seat — sales, account management, engineering leadership, delivery.

**Why it matters:** It corrects the assumption that a team automatically makes
things easier. Knowing the growing pains lets you build SOPs and systems ahead of
the project size you're chasing, rather than firefighting after.

**The moves:**
1. Recognize the stages: freelancer → consultant → agency with real systems.
2. Expect that scaling adds management overhead before it adds ease.
3. Build SOPs and internal systems so senior people aren't stuck in the weeds.
4. Put each role in its right seat as you grow (sales, account management, eng
   leadership, delivery).

**Watch out for:** You don't need a co-founder or full team to land sizeable
projects — but running an agency is a genuinely different, harder game than
freelancing. Inbound flooding in without SOPs creates chaos, not just growth.

**Original example to invent:** Source recounted a team's scaling arc. Writers
should illustrate the stages and role-fit with a different invented team.
