# Frontier coding models become better hackers by default

**What:** The claim that as coding models improve, they don't just find individual
vulnerabilities — they can chain several small ones into a full attack, the way
elite human security researchers do, and this capability will keep scaling across
every lab's frontier models.

**Why it matters:** It reframes AI security risk as a moving, compounding trend
rather than a single scary release. What a top model can do today, smaller and
open-source models are expected to do within a year or two, so the capability
diffuses and can't be "uninvented" or kept secret indefinitely.

**The moves:**
1. Plan on the assumption that exploit-finding capability will diffuse downward to
   cheaper and open models over time.
2. Distinguish single-bug discovery from multi-step exploit chaining — the latter is
   the more consequential capability.
3. Track how each new model generation shifts the offense/defense balance.

**Watch out for:** Treating this as hype or a one-time event underestimates the
exponential trajectory. Equally, panic is unwarranted if defenders are given the same
capability first (see the staged-deployment card).

**Original example to invent:** Discuss exploit-chaining abstractly with a different
scenario than the source's cited software.
