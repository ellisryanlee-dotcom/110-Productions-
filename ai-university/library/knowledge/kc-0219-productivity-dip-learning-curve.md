# Productivity dips before it climbs (the adoption J-curve)

**What:** A model for what happens when you switch to an AI-first way of working:
expect a temporary drop in output (the source estimates roughly a 20% dip) while you
adjust, because learning is exponential rather than linear. You fall behind the old
way for a few days, hit break-even, then pull sharply ahead.

**Why it matters:** People quit in the dip — they feel less productive during the
adjustment and conclude the change isn't worth it, right before the payoff. Naming
the dip in advance keeps you (and a team) from abandoning a change that pays a large
multiple once the curve turns up.

**The moves:**
1. Before adopting, accept a short-term productivity decrease as the cost of change.
2. Judge the change by whether the eventual upside exceeds the temporary dip.
3. Push through the days where you're below the old baseline — that gap is where
   people give up.
4. Expect exponential, not linear, improvement: break-even in days, then rapid gains.

**Watch out for:** The dip is real and demoralizing; without expecting it you may
mistake it for failure. The claimed magnitudes (a ~20% dip, ~50% eventual gain) are
illustrative, not measured — use them as intuition, not as promised numbers.

**Original example to invent:** Source framed a solo operator's 30-day sprint. Writers
should illustrate the J-curve with a different adoption scenario (e.g., a team
switching a core workflow).
