# Direct agents by outcomes, not step-by-step execution

**What:** A stance for running an agent organization: set high-level direction and
review outcomes rather than executing tasks yourself. You supply high-level goals,
target metrics, and the outcomes you want, then let a lead agent (e.g., a CEO agent) figure out who to hire, how to
sequence work, and which specialists to loop in. You interact by creating issues,
leaving comments, and approving decisions — not by micromanaging each step.

**Why it matters:** This is the leverage of agent orchestration: one person can direct
an entire "company" of agents by setting direction and reviewing results instead of
executing tasks. It also scales — you can automate a whole business, or peel off one
department and make it agent-first while leaving the rest human.

**The moves:**
1. Give the lead agent high-level goals, metrics, and desired outcomes — not step-by-step
   instructions.
2. Let it plan, hire/spawn the needed roles, and delegate.
3. Interact through issues and comments; approve or redirect at decision points.
4. Optionally require approval for consequential moves (e.g., new hires) or turn that
   off to let the org grow autonomously.

**Watch out for:** Board-level direction only works if the agents are competent and
well-instructed; vague goals with no review produces drift. Turning off approvals
increases autonomy and cost/risk simultaneously.
