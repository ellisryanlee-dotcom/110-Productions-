# Five beginner-friendly AI business models

**What:** A menu of five distinct ways an individual can earn money from the AI wave, each with a different effort profile, income ceiling, and skill payoff: (1) AI influencer/creator — explain AI simply on YouTube/social, monetized by ad revenue, affiliate links, and sponsorships; (2) AI consulting — sell advice calls to businesses and individuals wanting to adopt AI; (3) AI automation agency (AAA) — implement AI tools/systems for client businesses, typically with no-code/low-code; (4) AI education — package hard-won knowledge into courses, ebooks, or cohorts; (5) AI SaaS — build a software product businesses subscribe to.

**Why it matters:** Beginners waste time picking the wrong vehicle. Comparing the five on startup cost, speed to first dollar, income ceiling, and transferable skills lets someone match a model to their situation and sequence them over time (e.g., agency work generates the real-world results that later feed an education product or reveal a SaaS opportunity).

**The moves (how to compare them):**
1. Rank each by startup cost: influencer/consulting are near-zero; agency needs a few tool subscriptions; SaaS needs the most upfront capital and build time.
2. Rank by time-to-revenue: consulting is fastest; influencing needs months of consistent content first; SaaS is slowest.
3. Rank by income ceiling: agency and SaaS scale highest; consulting is capped by hours sold.
4. Note the skill payoff: agency work teaches the most durable, in-demand implementation skills.
5. Note the failure modes: influencing needs on-camera consistency; education requires you to have genuinely done the thing; SaaS can be made obsolete overnight by a model-provider release.

**Watch out for:** Consulting trades time for money and can stall growth. Education without real results collapses fast. SaaS carries platform risk — a foundation-model update can erase your product's edge. Influencing is slow to monetize and demands sustained output.
