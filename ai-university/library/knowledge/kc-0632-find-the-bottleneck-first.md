# Find the real bottleneck before you automate anything

**What:** A consulting method for locating where to apply automation: identify the
single constraint that would break first under load, and fix that — not whatever the
owner assumes the problem is. A powerful discovery question is to ask what would break
first if a large surge of new customers showed up tomorrow. Walking the operation
step by step under that hypothetical exposes where things actually fall apart (intake,
follow-up speed, reporting, fulfillment).

**Why it matters:** Businesses routinely pour more resources — ads, salespeople — into
a system that's already constrained upstream, which wastes money. The highest-leverage
work is removing the earliest constraint so flow improves; only after that does adding
more volume pay off. This also tends to surface a different, more valuable project than
the one the client requested.

**The moves:**
1. Ask the surge question: "If a large wave of new clients arrived tomorrow, what
   breaks first?"
2. Have the owner walk their operation end to end; note each point of failure.
3. Prioritize the earliest/biggest constraint as the first automation.
4. Sequence later work: clear the constraint, then help drive more volume through.

**Watch out for:** Don't accept the client's self-diagnosis or their requested
solution at face value — the stated ask and the true constraint often differ. Adding
volume before removing the constraint amplifies the problem instead of the results.

**Original example to invent:** Source frames flow with a clogged-pipe analogy.
Writers must express the constraint-first idea without that analogy and with a fresh
discovery walkthrough that surfaces an unexpected bottleneck.
