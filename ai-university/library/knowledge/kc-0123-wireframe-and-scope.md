# Wireframe first, scope honestly, scale vertically

**What:** A set of hard-won practices for turning automation ideas into deliverable systems: plan on a canvas before building, question whether AI is even needed, and grow a system deep before wide.

**Why it matters:** Most published "agents" are demos, not production-ready templates — pushing them to real users exposes edge cases that break them. Beginners who plan, right-size AI, and scale carefully ship far more reliable work, whether for themselves or clients.

**The moves:**
1. Wireframe before building: sketch trigger → data flow → decisions → actions → integrations first; break the whole into small workflows and tasks. Spend more time planning than in the builder.
2. Ask "do we even need AI here?" Rule-based routing handles fixed categories; reserve AI for steps that require reading meaning, reasoning, or dynamic decisions.
3. Build plain workflows first, add AI to specific steps, and escalate to agents only when dynamic tool-calling is genuinely required.
4. Feed the system good context (prompt + memory + retrieval) — garbage in, garbage out.
5. Scale vertically: perfect one end-to-end use case with evaluations, monitoring, and guardrails before expanding to new areas or many users.
6. Accept no-code limits: it's excellent for fast builds, integrations, and internal automations, but enterprise-scale data, auth, and complex tool-calling may need a hybrid of no-code plus custom code.

**Watch out for:** Skipping the plan leads to bloated flows and hours of rebuilding. Scaling horizontally too early multiplies hallucinations, slows retrieval, and surfaces untested edge cases. Even large, well-resourced companies struggle with AI reliability — set expectations accordingly.

**Original example to invent:** The source compares planning to following an instruction manual before assembling parts, and giving an agent context to onboarding a new hire. Describe these ideas abstractly and invent your own analogies so writers don't copy the manual/intern framing.
