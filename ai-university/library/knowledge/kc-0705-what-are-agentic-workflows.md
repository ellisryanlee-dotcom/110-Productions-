# What agentic workflows are: declare outcomes, not steps

**What:** A shift in how automations are built. In the traditional model you
specify *how* — every node, connection, variable, and condition. In an agentic
workflow you specify *what* you want in plain language (where data comes from,
what should happen, where it ends up) and the agent figures out the steps. The
mental model is directing a capable human developer: you describe the outcome,
constraints, and available tools, and they build it.

**Why it matters:** It moves your effort from constructing every piece to
defining the goal clearly. The scarce skill becomes communicating a precise
scope, not wiring integrations. It also raises a hard requirement: if you can't
articulate what you want, no human or agent can build it.

**The moves:**
1. Describe the outcome, inputs, tools, and what "done" looks like — in words.
2. Let the agent propose the implementation instead of pre-designing nodes.
3. Use the agent itself to brainstorm and sharpen the scope; it will surface
   questions you hadn't considered.
4. Keep thinking — you still own the plan; you're delegating the build, not the
   judgment.

**Watch out for:** This is not fully hands-off "vibe" building; a vague brief
produces a vague system. Clarity of scope is now the bottleneck.

**Original example to invent:** Take a mundane task and write two briefs for it —
one step-by-step "how" brief and one outcome-only "what" brief — and discuss how
each would steer a build.
