# The three ROI levers every pitch should move

**What:** Business owners evaluate an automation on three questions: will it save
time, will it make or save money, and will it be more consistent / higher quality
than the current manual process. A pitch should clearly show improvement on at least
two of the three; hitting all three makes the decision a no-brainer.

**Why it matters:** Owners don't think in workflow complexity; they think in returns.
Framing every proposal against these levers speaks their language and makes the value
legible without any technical explanation.

**The moves:**
1. For the target process, quantify current time cost.
2. Quantify the money impact — hours saved translated to dollars, or revenue
   recovered/created.
3. Assess whether the automated version is more consistent or higher quality.
4. Build the offer so it visibly wins on two-plus levers; state the upfront and
   ongoing price against that combined return.

**Watch out for:** "Faster" alone is a weak pitch — speed without a money or quality
story rarely justifies a premium. Make sure the improvements you claim are ones the
owner can verify against their own operation.

**Original example to invent:** Source sketched an offer framed as monthly time and
dollar savings. Writers should invent a different service and show it scoring on each
of the three levers explicitly.
