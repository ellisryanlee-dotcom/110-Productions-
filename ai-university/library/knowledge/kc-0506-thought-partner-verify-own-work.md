# Get more from an AI: interview mode, devil's advocate, and self-verification

**What:** Three usage habits that raise the quality and trustworthiness of AI output: (1) use it as a *thought partner* rather than an oracle, (2) have it *interview you* to extract knowledge, and (3) make it *verify its own work* before handing it back.

**Why it matters:** Models tend to please the user (sycophancy) and will confidently state wrong things. Left unchecked, first-pass output lands maybe partway there and you iterate manually. Building in verification pushes the first pass much closer to done and lets you trust outputs more.

**The moves:**
1. **Thought partner:** brainstorm with it, ask it to play devil's advocate, and take its answers with a grain of salt; optionally spin up several agents/personas to argue different perspectives, then decide with your own judgment.
2. **Interview you:** run an interview-style prompt that relentlessly asks questions until it has enough to capture what's in your head — a fast way to seed a knowledge base or plan a project.
3. **Verify its work:** give the agent the ability to check itself the way a human reviewer would — open a browser and click through, take screenshots of the result, or test that different personas (beginner, engineer, business owner) can use it. Only accept work after it has self-checked.
4. When it gets something wrong, have it fix the underlying instruction/skill and show you the exact source of any data it cites.

**Watch out for:** Sycophancy means "looks confident" is not "is correct." Verification must be an explicit step you require, not something you assume happened.

**Original example to invent:** The source verifies an interactive artifact by clicking through it. Writers should invent a different deliverable (e.g., a pricing sheet) and show a concrete self-verification loop for it.
