# The early-mover window on new platforms

**What:** When a genuinely new platform or technology emerges, there's a temporary window where attention and demand vastly exceed the supply of people who can build for it, making outsized returns unusually achievable. As the ecosystem matures and supply catches up, that window closes and returns normalize. The strategic implication: move early on new AI platforms to capture disproportionate upside.

**Why it matters:** Timing, not just talent, drives who profits from a technology wave. Recognizing an early window tells you when to move aggressively — and recognizing a saturated one tells you when a "guaranteed" opportunity has already passed.

**The reasoning:**
1. New platforms concentrate attention, funding, and buyer urgency while few suppliers exist.
2. Early builders face low competition and often low costs (e.g., cheap early ad inventory, uncontested app categories).
3. Historical parallels across prior platform shifts (early web, mobile app stores, early ad platforms, early short-form video) show early participants captured outsized gains.
4. Over time, imitation and rising costs compress margins toward equilibrium.

**Watch out for:** "Early = easy" is a probability boost, not a guarantee; survivorship bias inflates the highlight stories. The same logic warns that a widely hyped, already-crowded opportunity is likely late, not early.
