# The five levels of Claude mastery (enthusiast → architect)

**What:** A maturity model describing five progressive stages of using an AI assistant, each with a characteristic capability set and a "ceiling" you must break through to advance. Level 1 (enthusiast): one-off Q&A in chat. Level 2 (beginner): projects, memory, connectors, file creation. Level 3 (intermediate): the assistant operating your computer with file access. Level 4 (advanced): a coding harness running parallel specialized sessions. Level 5 (architect): autonomous, cloud-run systems that work while you're away.

**Why it matters:** It gives learners a map — where they are, what's blocking them, and the specific "cheat code" to reach the next level. It also reframes progress as adopting new *surfaces* (chat → computer-use → coding harness → cloud automation), each unlocking more leverage (roughly: 30 min/day saved at L1 up to always-on infrastructure at L5).

**The moves:**
1. Identify your current level by the capabilities you actually use.
2. Break each ceiling with a targeted next step: L1→L2 create your first project; L2→L3 open a computer-use surface; L3→L4 set up a reliable folder structure the agent relies on; L4→L5 automate your single most repetitive weekly task.
3. Recognize each level's ceiling — e.g., chat can't act on your machine; manual parallel work makes *you* the bottleneck at L4.
4. Progress by adoption and trust, not just features.

**Watch out for:** Many people stall for non-technical reasons (fear of anything technical, or unwillingness to hand off control). Higher levels raise capability but also cost and risk; don't skip the trust-building. (Specific product names/dates in the source are fast-moving — verify before repeating.)

**Original example to invent:** The source narrates its own five-level journey. Writers should invent a persona and walk them up two adjacent levels with a concrete ceiling and cheat-code for each.
