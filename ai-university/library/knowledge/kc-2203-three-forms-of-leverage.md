# The three forms of leverage (applied to AI)

**What:** A borrowed framework (attributed to a well-known technologist-investor) that leverage — multiplying the impact of your effort without a matching increase in time or resources — comes in three forms: labor (other people acting on your instructions), capital (money amplifying a decision), and products with no marginal cost of replication, which split into code and content. The AI-agency thesis is powerful because it stacks several of these at once.

**Why it matters:** It explains why the AI-service opportunity is unusually high-leverage: you use code (building an AI system that performs work) to create labor leverage (a digital worker replacing headcount), and then use content leverage (videos, posts, articles) to market the whole thing — all with permissionless, low-cost tools.

**The moves:**
1. Recognize which form of leverage each activity gives you.
2. Prefer activities that combine forms — e.g., a reusable automation (code→labor) promoted through content.
3. Favor content and code as the beginner-accessible forms, since they don't require capital or a team to start.

**Watch out for:** Leverage amplifies outcomes in both directions; a flawed system or message scales its flaws too. Content leverage only pays off with consistent output over time, not a single post.

**Original example to invent:** The source paraphrases a specific thinker's taxonomy and cites named investors for the capital example. Writers should re-derive the labor/capital/code/content distinction in their own words and use fresh examples (not the specific people named).
