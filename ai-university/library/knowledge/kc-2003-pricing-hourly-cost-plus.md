# Beginner pricing: hourly and cost-plus developer markup

**What:** Two entry-level ways to price AI service projects. (1) Hourly: estimate the hours a project takes, set a personal hourly rate, and bill hours × rate — appropriate when you deliver the work yourself, essentially as a freelancer. (2) Cost-plus with a subcontracted developer: if you don't do the technical work, estimate total hours, pay a developer their hourly rate, and charge the client roughly 2–3× what you pay the developer, so the markup covers your margin without you building anything.

**Why it matters:** New agency owners freeze on pricing. These two models give a defensible starting number and let a non-technical founder still profit by orchestrating a developer rather than coding.

**The moves:**
1. Scope the project into an hours estimate.
2. For hourly: pick a rate reflecting your skill level and multiply.
3. For cost-plus: find a developer, learn their hourly cost, then quote the client a 2–3× multiple of that cost.
4. Ensure the multiple covers your time managing the project, revisions, and risk — not just the raw dev cost.

**Watch out for:** Hourly caps your income at hours-in-the-day and penalizes you for getting faster. Cost-plus depends on accurate hour estimates — underestimating erodes the margin. Both are starting points; value-based pricing and retainers scale better once you have proof (see related pricing cards).
