# The silent killers: underpricing, under-scoping, chasing small retainers

**What:** Three traps that quietly destroy an automation business. Underpricing
attracts the wrong clients and makes future rate increases nearly impossible.
Under-scoping — agreeing to every "small additional feature" while never redrawing
the scope — obliterates margins. Chasing many small monthly retainers too early is worse for money, mental
health, and reputation than landing fewer larger projects.

**Why it matters:** Each trap feels like progress (a sale, a happy client, recurring
revenue) but compounds into low margins and a business that's hard to grow. Naming
them lets you avoid the decisions that cap your ceiling.

**The moves:**
1. Price to attract good clients and preserve room to raise rates later.
2. Define exactly what's included up front and run a change-request process for
   anything outside scope.
3. Prefer one larger project over several tiny retainers early on; stability comes
   from a lead pipeline, not from retainers.
4. Revisit rates regularly (tie to the close-rate signal in kc-0343).

**Watch out for:** "One more small feature" without a rescope is the most common
margin leak. Early retainer-chasing spreads you thin for little profit — build the
pipeline instead.
