# The transition curve: expect the emotional dip when learning something new

**What:** A model of the emotional stages people move through when learning something hard. You start as an optimist excited by the opportunity, then drop into pessimism as you grasp the real complexity and feel overwhelmed, then hit a low decision point where you either quit or push through, and — if you push through — climb back to an informed, realistic optimism. Being aware of the curve in advance makes the dip survivable.

**Why it matters:** Everyone feels overwhelmed early when confronted with unfamiliar concepts (data formats, requests, prompting agents). If you expect the dip and recognize it as a normal stage rather than a sign you can't do it, you're far more likely to persist to competence. The cycle also repeats — you re-enter the pessimism stage many times as you tackle new things.

**The moves:**
1. Set expectations up front: early overwhelm is a phase, not a verdict.
2. When you hit the low point, recognize it as the decision stage and choose to push through.
3. Use the momentum of the dip to climb toward informed optimism rather than quitting.
4. Accept that you'll cycle through this repeatedly with each new challenge.

**Watch out for:** Mistaking the normal pessimism dip for evidence you're not cut out for it is how people quit right before it clicks. (This is a motivational model, hence lower confidence as a factual claim.)
