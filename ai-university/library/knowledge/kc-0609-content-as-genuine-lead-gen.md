# Publish to attract the genuinely interested, not to go viral

**What:** Consistently sharing what you're building and learning — on video, a blog,
LinkedIn, or a community — attracts the small number of people who care about exactly
what you care about. Early clients often come from these low-view, high-intent
audiences, sometimes just reaching out to talk before any pitch exists.

**Why it matters:** You don't need scale or an expert persona to land first work. Raw
authenticity builds the trust that makes people want to work with you. The goal of
the content isn't reach; it's connection with the few who are ready to buy or
collaborate.

**The moves:**
1. Pick a platform and post what you're actually learning and building.
2. Make it easy to contact you (a visible email or link), even without a hard CTA.
3. Optimize for genuine interest and clarity, not view counts.
4. Take the conversations that come in — treat early ones as relationship-building,
   not just sales calls.

**Watch out for:** Positioning yourself as a polished "expert" too early can reduce
the authenticity that actually converts. Chasing virality attracts a broad, low-intent
crowd instead of the handful who will pay.

**Original example to invent:** Source recounts a viewer becoming a paying client
after a casual call. Writers should invent a different origin story for a first client
that keeps the "genuine-interest-over-reach" principle intact.
