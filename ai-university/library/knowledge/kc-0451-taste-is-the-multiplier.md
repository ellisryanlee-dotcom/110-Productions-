# Taste is the multiplier; know the current limits

**What:** AI video tools amplify the operator's creative judgment rather than supplying it. People who already understand what makes video engaging — scenes, transitions, pacing — get 10x productivity; people without that sense get flat, static, generic outputs. The tools also have real current limits: they can't hear/read a video's audio on their own, output can drift into static, energy-less stretches, and short-form work isn't yet reliably good enough to publish.

**Why it matters:** Setting expectations correctly prevents both disappointment ("the tool is bad") and overconfidence ("it replaces editors"). The realistic use is a force-multiplier for people with taste and direction.

**The moves:**
1. Bring your own creative direction — pacing, transitions, what to emphasize.
2. Expect strong results on structured, branded pieces and weaker results where nuance and energy matter most (like scroll-stopping short-form).
3. Watch for static drift and re-inject energy through feedback.
4. Treat outputs as fast first drafts to steer, not finished products.

**Watch out for:** Without human taste, you get outputs that are technically animated but lifeless. Some formats (attention-grabbing shorts) may not yet meet a publishable bar without heavy iteration.
