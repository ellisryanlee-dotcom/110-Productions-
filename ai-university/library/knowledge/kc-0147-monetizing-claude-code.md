# Monetizing agentic-automation skills

**What:** A positioning and pricing framework for selling automation work. Since building is getting commoditized, the value is in diagnosing the real constraint in a business and pricing on the value delivered, not hours — plus treating the tool's ~$100–200/month cost as employing a software engineer rather than a subscription.

**Why it matters:** Competing on "I build automations" is a race to the bottom. Businesses pay for outcomes (time saved, errors removed, revenue gained), and value-based pricing captures that upside while hourly pricing caps your income.

**The moves:**
1. Diagnose before prescribing: study the client's operations to find the actual bottleneck rather than building the flashy thing they asked for.
2. Lead with outcomes ("save X hours/month," "cut errors X%"), not the tool or the build.
3. Price on value: if a system saves $10K/month, a $5K build is a no-brainer for them and a great margin for you.
4. Use hourly only early to build trust/wins; switch to value pricing once you can show ROI.
5. Stay after delivery, track the metrics yourself, and expand — turning a small build into an ongoing relationship (freelancer → consultant → trusted partner).
6. Understand the deploy caveat when quoting reliability: deployed workflows are deterministic and don't self-heal.

**Watch out for:** Most demos are proofs of concept, not production-ready — set client expectations. Skill/template selling is a real but likely short-lived moment; don't bank a business on it.
