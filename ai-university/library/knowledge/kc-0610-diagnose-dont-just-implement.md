# Become the diagnostician, not just the implementer

**What:** The higher-value position is being someone who can identify *what's wrong*
in a business and where automation should be applied — not merely someone who builds
whatever the client already asked for. Many owners know they should be using AI but
have no idea where in their operation to start.

**Why it matters:** Anyone can be handed a spec and build it. The person who can walk
in, understand the operation, and surface the real problem is far scarcer and
commands more trust and higher fees. Diagnosis is where you differentiate from
commodity builders.

**The moves:**
1. Before proposing a build, learn how the business actually runs and where time and
   money leak.
2. Ask questions that expose bottlenecks rather than assuming the client's stated
   request is the real need.
3. Recommend the highest-leverage fix, which may differ from what they came in asking
   for.
4. Frame yourself around problem-finding, so clients bring you ambiguity, not just
   tickets.

**Watch out for:** Taking the client's self-diagnosis at face value can lead you to
build the wrong thing well. The stated request and the actual constraint are often
different.

**Original example to invent:** Source contrasts a fixer with a diagnostician using a
medical metaphor. Writers must express the idea without that metaphor and with a fresh
discovery scenario.
