# Become tool-agnostic; sell the outcome, not the platform

**What:** A positioning mindset: clients hire you for results — time saved, money
saved, problems solved — not for a specific tool. So the goal is to understand
where AI is and isn't valuable and to solve the core business problem, treating
the platform as an interchangeable means to that end.

**Why it matters:** The space churns constantly ("what dropped, what's relevant,
what do I learn?"). Anchoring your identity to one tool makes every new release
feel like a threat. Anchoring to problem-solving makes tool changes irrelevant
and keeps you sellable across shifts in the ecosystem.

**The moves:**
1. Start from the business problem and its cost, not from a tool you like.
2. Learn *when* AI is worth applying and when it isn't, before learning any
   platform deeply.
3. Treat platforms as swappable; pick per use case rather than by loyalty.
4. In sales, lead with the outcome and ROI; keep the tool a footnote.

**Watch out for:** Tool tribalism and fear of missing the "next big thing" both
distract from the durable skill (diagnosing and solving problems). Don't let a
platform's marketing set your roadmap.

**Original example to invent:** Show one business problem solved with two
completely different toolchains to make the point that the outcome, not the
platform, is what the client buys.
