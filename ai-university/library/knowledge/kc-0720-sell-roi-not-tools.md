# Sell ROI, not tools: quantify time saved into dollars

**What:** The core selling move across every stage: clients pay for measurable
outcomes, not for a template or a platform. You make the value obvious by doing
the math — estimate how long a task currently takes, how much of it you can
automate, and convert that into hours and dollars saved over a month and a year.
A small customization that lands a client is valuable because of the outcome it
delivers, not the tool behind it.

**Why it matters:** Showing savings in black and white turns your fee from a cost
into an investment with a return, which justifies premium and value-based pricing.
It also protects you from tool churn — the pitch is the result, not the software.

**The moves:**
1. Measure the current cost of the process (hours × frequency × labor rate).
2. Estimate the fraction you can automate and the resulting time saved.
3. Convert to monthly and annual dollar savings and present it plainly.
4. Price against that value so the ROI makes your fee an easy yes.
5. Capture before/after numbers as a case study for the next client.

**Watch out for:** Don't lead with the tool or the build; lead with the number.
Also include intangibles (employee quality of life, faster response times), but
anchor on the quantified savings.

**Original example to invent:** Take a fictional manual task, run the hours→dollars
calculation, and write the one-sentence ROI line you'd say on a sales call.
