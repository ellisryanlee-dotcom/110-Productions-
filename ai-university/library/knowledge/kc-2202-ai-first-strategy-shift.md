# The "AI-first" business strategy shift

**What:** The argument that businesses are moving from treating AI as a bolt-on feature to making it the core of how the business operates — analogous to an earlier platform shift where companies reprioritized around a newly dominant channel. Because an AI system that performs a human task can be duplicated at near-zero marginal cost, an AI-first business can scale capacity (sales, support, delivery) without proportionally scaling headcount.

**Why it matters:** It frames a durable demand thesis: the companies that can help others re-architect around AI will be in high demand for a long time, while laggard businesses risk being out-competed. It also points to three ways to profit from the shift.

**The moves (three ways to act on it):**
1. Build and sell AI systems to existing businesses to help them become AI-first (the agency route).
2. Start a brand-new business designed AI-first from day one, aiming to out-scale incumbents in a traditional industry.
3. Partner with an existing business to rebuild it AI-first in exchange for equity or a revenue/profit share.
4. Document and market whatever you build with content, to become discoverable.

**Watch out for:** This is a forward-looking thesis, not a demonstrated result — the "infinitely scalable one-person company" idea is aspirational framing. The near-zero-marginal-cost logic applies cleanly to software/content, less cleanly to services that still need human oversight.

**Original example to invent:** The source leans on a well-known investor's public bet about future company sizes. Writers should make the scalability point with their own worked example (e.g., one support agent serving many customers) rather than reusing that reference.
