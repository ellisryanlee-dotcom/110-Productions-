# You don't need to be an expert — solving a real problem gets paid

**What:** The claim is that you can be paid well for stitching together existing,
off-the-shelf AI tools to solve a real business problem — you don't need to be a
coder, genius, or credentialed expert. The market is still early: many owners are
only now discovering what's possible, and there's broad demand for high-leverage
automations like personalized outreach, customer support, and productivity systems.

**Why it matters:** This reframes the opportunity for builders who feel unqualified.
Buyers care about the problem being solved and the value delivered, not about which
tools you used or how sophisticated the internals are.

**The moves:**
1. Get comfortable identifying pain points, then match them to tools you already know.
2. Don't over-invest in being cutting-edge; combining accessible tools well is enough
   for most small-business needs.
3. Target owners who want to grow without adding headcount.
4. Broaden your tool literacy so you can pick the simplest thing that solves the
   problem, not just the one framework you know.

**Watch out for:** This is an asserted market read, not a demonstrated result — the
space also evolves fast, so specific "easy money" tactics age quickly. The durable
part is "solve a real problem," not any particular tool or niche.

**Original example to invent:** Source lists outreach, support, and productivity as
categories. Writers should pick one under-served niche and describe a concrete
off-the-shelf solution a non-expert could deliver.
