# Pick automations with compounding ROI, not personal assistants

**What:** A prioritization rule for which automation to build first for a client.
Systems whose usage grows as the business grows — lead response, lead nurture,
customer support — deliver ROI that compounds: as volume rises, the system runs
more often and saves more. A personal assistant for one owner does not scale the
same way, because more business does not necessarily mean more assistant usage.

**Why it matters:** Clients often *ask* for the flashy personal assistant, but
the fastest, most defensible ROI comes from throughput systems tied to revenue.
Leading with a compounding system shows value quickly and grows with the account.

**The moves:**
1. When scoping, ask which processes scale in volume as the business grows.
2. Favor a first build that sits on a revenue-driving, high-throughput process.
3. Frame the pitch around a fast, visible quick win rather than a broad
   "assistant for everything."
4. Save personal-productivity automations for later, once trust and ROI proof
   exist.

**Watch out for:** The client's stated request (often a personal assistant) may
not be their highest-leverage opportunity; redirect gently toward the
compounding system. Don't oversell scope before proving one win.

**Original example to invent:** Contrast two candidate first-builds for a fictional
business — one throughput system tied to revenue and one owner-convenience tool —
and walk through how each scales over a year of growth.
