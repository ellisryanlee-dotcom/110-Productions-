# Sell the outcome, and pick a lane: specialist or consultant

**What:** Two coupled ideas for going to market. First, sell the outcome (save hours,
cut errors, respond to leads faster), not the mechanism (a workflow or a specific
tool) — outcomes are what people pay for. Second, choose a path: (a) niche down and
become the definitive expert on one specific process, or (b) go broad as a consultant
who understands many use cases and diagnoses the right fix.

**Why it matters:** Leading with the workflow makes you a commodity; leading with the
outcome makes you valuable. And the specialist-vs-consultant choice shapes everything
— the specialist learns one problem deeply (better case studies, positioning,
testimonials, and pricing power), while the consultant's value is breadth and
diagnosis. A practical on-ramp is to pick one automation, learn it inside out, build a
simple version, and demo it.

**The moves:**
1. Frame offers as time saved / money made / mistakes removed, not as "a workflow."
2. Decide your path: deep specialist in one process, or broad diagnostic consultant.
3. If specializing, learn the language, objections, and failure points of that one
   process so you can promise better results.
4. Start by mastering a single automation and showing an owner a working demo.

**Watch out for:** Trying to learn and offer everything at once dilutes your
positioning and pricing power — depth in one lane usually beats a shallow menu of ten
services. The specialist can charge more precisely because they're known for one thing.
