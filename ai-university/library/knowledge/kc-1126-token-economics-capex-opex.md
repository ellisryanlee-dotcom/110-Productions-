# Token economics: capex vs opex of vibe vs agentic coding

**What:** A cost framing borrowed from capital vs operating expenditure. Vibe coding is low capex (no time spent designing a harness) but high opex — you burn through huge volumes of tokens iterating on low-quality output because the agent has no system to follow. Agentic engineering is high capex (you invest up front building the harness, sometimes via a small dedicated team) but low opex, because output quality is higher and improves over time.

**Why it matters:** It quantifies why building the harness is worth it: the crossover where agentic engineering becomes cheaper than vibe coding arrives quickly, and past it agentic work is reportedly several times more reliable and cheaper. The upfront investment amortizes fast and scales across an organization.

**The moves:**
1. For anything beyond throwaway work, treat harness-building as capex worth paying.
2. Expect vibe coding to feel cheaper at first, then bleed tokens on rework.
3. In a larger org, use a small forward-deployed team to build the harness, then scale it out.
4. Reinvest saved opex into evolving the system.

**Watch out for:** Judging a method by its day-one cost misleads — vibe coding's low entry cost hides its ongoing token burn. The exact crossover point and multiples are estimates.
